
The core model holds up, but I found four citation or number errors, several claims that run ahead of the sources, and some gaps in the clock model, which is your central thesis. Most of this is checked against primary-source summaries. I'm not a tax professional, so treat it as a pre-CPA pass.

**Errors to fix**

1. **§3.3 catch-up limit.** The 2026 401(k) catch-up is $8,000, so the age 50–59/64+ total is $24,500 + $8,000 = **$32,500**, not $32,000. Your $35,750 for ages 60–63 is correct.
2. **§3.1 phase-out citation.** The numbers are right, but the Roth phase-out ranges come from Notice 2025-67, not Rev. Proc. 2025-32. I didn't open Rev. Proc. 2025-32 itself, so confirm it doesn't also list them.
3. **§3.2 TCJA block quote isn't verbatim.** The conference report acknowledges the strategy in footnotes 268, 269, 276 and 277, so cite more than 268. The footnote's actual wording differs from yours. It refers to AGI exceeding certain limits and uses "is not permitted" and "can", and has no "then." Paste the exact text.
4. **Reg lettering in §3 and §3.3.** Treas. Reg. § 1.408A-10 A-3(a) covers both cases: the investment-in-the-contract portion is a regular contribution and the rest is earnings, and a qualified distribution rolled over is entirely regular contribution. A-3(b) is the edge case where the investment in the contract exceeds the account balance. Your "Q&A-3(b)" for non-qualified rollovers should be 3(a).

**Claims stronger than the sources**

5. **Step-transaction ("the IRS cannot apply…").** Conference report footnotes are legislative history, not statute. An IRS official indicated in 2018 that the agency accepts the technique, citing that footnote, but I found no formal guidance. Better wording: "strongest available support," not "cannot."
6. **"Strictly superior" Mega-first insight (§3.3).** "Often strictly superior" contradicts itself. The Tier 1 argument only matters if you withdraw early. The pollution argument is the real one, and it applies when you hold pre-tax IRA balances. Mega-first also carries plan-side friction: fund menu and fees, plus availability and 401(m) testing. I'd make the advice conditional.
7. **Pro-rata example wording.** "Tax on dollars you already paid" isn't quite right. The leftover basis isn't lost. It stays on Form 8606 and is recovered later. What happens is that you convert a pro-rata slice of pre-tax money and pay tax on it now.
8. **§1.1 qualified exit.** Age 59½ isn't the only trigger. Death, disability, and a first-time home purchase also qualify. The "every state with an income tax" claim is a blanket statement I couldn't verify, so soften it.

**Clock model gaps**

9. **Clock count.** §2.1 defines Clock C as the overlay for taxable in-plan rollovers, but §3.3 relies on "5 years in plan." That is the Roth 401(k)'s own per-plan qualification period (§ 402A(d)(2)), which is separate from Clock B. It also doesn't tack onto the Roth IRA: the Roth IRA's period starts at the earlier of its normal start year or the year of the designated-Roth rollover. Either you have four clocks or Clock C needs a broader definition. Your qualified/non-qualified branches also skip age ≥ 59½ with under 5 years in plan, which is non-qualified.
10. **§3.5 "Recapture Clock Armed? None."** That's true for Clock A, but Clock B starts with the first regular contribution year or, if earlier, the first conversion year. The Backdoor and Mega adapters can start it too. A Clock B column would fix this.
11. **Accrued-earnings option (§3.3).** You say converting earnings creates a Tier 2a tranche. Under your own Tier 1 logic, in-plan converted amounts rolled to a Roth IRA would land in Tier 1 with the Clock C overlay. Tier 2a applies only on the direct-to-IRA path. Also, the Tier 1 treatment of designated Roth basis is well supported, but in-plan rollover amounts are less settled. The Form 8606 instructions count designated-Roth rollover amounts treated as investment in the contract as regular contribution basis, while some practitioners argue in-plan rollover amounts should be conversion basis. Say so, since you're inviting CPA review.
